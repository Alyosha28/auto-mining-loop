Language models can modify the procedures used to conduct research, but separating model guidance from legal random search requires matched controls. This paper studies budget-bounded evolution of a factor-research harness through Propose--Probe--Compose continuation, atomic harness edits, and one final common-support comparison. Six fresh tasks, two development seeds, and two independent model-call replicates form 24 paired units, with four decisions per arm and at most twelve expression-candidate positions per decision. The memory-equipped process improved mean absolute IC over random legal edits, with median paired difference 0.003534 and six positive task medians, and over the fixed harness, with median difference 0.010977 and positive effects in all 24 units. Task-cluster intervals were [0.001623, 0.005119] and [0.008080, 0.015456], respectively. Cumulative memory did not show a stable increment over scalar feedback, and compact diagnostics did not show a stable increment over memory; both component contrasts had Holm-adjusted $p=1.0$. Relative to scalar feedback and random legal edits, memory showed reduced novelty and increased duplication. The panel recorded 288 model calls and 347 executed decisions, retaining 37 failed or invalid decision records. These results provide confirmatory development-panel evidence for model-guided harness adaptation under the registered tasks and budget, while leaving the independent value of the added information channels unresolved.
